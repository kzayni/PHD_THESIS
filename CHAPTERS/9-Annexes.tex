\Annexe{THERMODYNAMIC BALANCE EQUATIONS}
\label{app:thermodynamic_model}

This appendix provides the detailed expressions associated with the mass- and energy-balance equations introduced in Section~\ref{sec:review_thermodynamics}. The balances are applied locally to a surface control volume containing the ice layer and, when present, a liquid-water film.

\section{Mass Balance}

The local conservation of water mass is expressed as

\begin{equation}
    \dot{m}_{imp}
    + \sum \dot{m}_{in}
    =
    \sum \dot{m}_{out}
    + \dot{m}_{ev/su}
    + \dot{m}_{ice}
    \label{eq:app_mass_balance}
\end{equation}

where $\dot{m}_{imp}$ is the impinging-water mass flow rate, $\dot{m}_{in}$ and $\dot{m}_{out}$ are the incoming and outgoing runback-water flow rates, respectively, $\dot{m}_{ev/su}$ represents mass loss through evaporation or sublimation, and $\dot{m}_{ice}$ is the ice-accretion rate. The evaporation or sublimation rate may be evaluated as

\begin{equation}
    \dot{m}_{ev/su}
    =
    \frac{0.7}{C_{p,a}}\,HTC
    \left(
        \frac{p_{v,s}-p_{v,\infty}}{P_\infty}
    \right)
    \label{eq:app_evaporation}
\end{equation}

where $C_{p,a}$ is the specific heat capacity of air, $p_{v,s}$ and $p_{v,\infty}$ are the water-vapor pressures at the surface and in the freestream, respectively, and $P_\infty$ is the freestream static pressure. The vapor pressure at the water or ice surface is evaluated as~\cite{ozgen2009ice}

\begin{equation}
    p_v
    =
    3386
    \left(
        0.0039
        + 6.8096 \times 10^{-6}\,\bar{T}^{\,2}
        + 3.5579 \times 10^{-7}\,\bar{T}^{\,3}
    \right)
    \label{eq:app_vapor_pressure}
\end{equation}

where $p_v$ is expressed in Pa and

\begin{equation}
    \bar{T}
    =
    72 + 1.8\left(T-273.15\right)
    \label{eq:app_vapor_temperature}
\end{equation}

with $T$ expressed in K.

\section{Energy Balance}

Conservation of energy over the same surface control volume is written as

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{kin}
    + \dot{Q}_{d}
    + \dot{Q}_{a}
    + \sum \dot{Q}_{in}
    =
    \dot{Q}_{conv}
    + \dot{Q}_{rad}
    + \dot{Q}_{ev/su}
    + \sum \dot{Q}_{out}
    + \dot{Q}_{ice}
    \label{eq:app_energy_balance}
\end{equation}

where the individual contributions are detailed below. The kinetic energy associated with the impinging droplets is

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{kin}
    =
    \dot{m}_{imp}\frac{u_d^2}{2}
    \label{eq:app_qkin}
\end{equation}

where $u_d$ is the droplet impact velocity. The sensible heat associated with the impinging droplets is

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{d}
    =
    \dot{m}_{imp} C_{p,w}
    \left(T_s-T_\infty\right),
    \label{eq:app_qdroplet}
\end{equation}

where $C_{p,w}$ is the specific heat capacity of liquid water. The aerodynamic-heating contribution is expressed as

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{a}
    =
    \frac{r\,HTC\,U_\infty^2}
         {2C_{p,a}},
    \label{eq:app_qaero}
\end{equation}

where $r$ is the recovery factor. The convective heat exchange is

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{conv}
    =
    HTC\left(T_s-T_{rec}\right),
    \label{eq:app_qconv_recovery}
\end{equation}

The energy transported by incoming and outgoing runback water is

\begin{equation}
    \sum \dot{Q}_{in}
    =
    \sum \dot{m}_{in} C_{p,w}
    \left(T_s-T_{ref}\right),
    \label{eq:app_qin}
\end{equation}

and

\begin{equation}
    \sum \dot{Q}_{out}
    =
    \sum \dot{m}_{out} C_{p,w}
    \left(T_s-T_{ref}\right),
    \label{eq:app_qout}
\end{equation}

respectively, where $T_{ref}$ denotes the reference freezing temperature. Radiative heat transfer is given by

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{rad}
    =
    4\epsilon\sigma_r T_\infty^3
    \left(T_s-T_\infty\right),
    \label{eq:app_qrad}
\end{equation}

where $\epsilon$ is the surface emissivity and $\sigma$ is the Stefan--Boltzmann constant. The latent-energy contributions associated with evaporation and sublimation are

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{ev}
    =
    \dot{m}_{ev} L_v,
    \qquad
    \dot{Q}_{su}
    =
    \dot{m}_{su} L_s,
    \label{eq:app_qevsu}
\end{equation}

where $L_v$ and $L_s$ are the latent heats of vaporization and
sublimation, respectively. The energy associated with ice formation may be expressed as

\begin{equation}
    \dot{Q}_{ice}
    =
    \dot{m}_{ice}
    \left[
        C_{p,i}\left(T_s-T_{ref}\right)-L_f
    \right],
    \label{eq:app_qice}
\end{equation}

where $C_{p,i}$ is the specific heat capacity of ice and $L_f$ is the latent heat of fusion.

\Annexe{NUMERICAL VERIFICATION}
\label{app:verification_validation}

This appendix provides supplementary verification results for the numerical methodologies developed in this thesis.

\section{Lagrangian Droplet Integration}
\label{app:lagrangian_integration}

The temporal accuracy of the Lagrangian droplet integration is assessed using a manufactured trajectory with an analytical solution. A droplet is transported in a prescribed uniform carrier velocity $\boldsymbol{U}$ and subjected to a constant body acceleration $\boldsymbol{g}$ according to
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\boldsymbol{u}_d}{dt}
    =
    2\left(\boldsymbol{U}-\boldsymbol{u}_d\right)+\boldsymbol{g}
    \qquad
    \frac{d\boldsymbol{x}_d}{dt}
    =
    \boldsymbol{u}_d
\end{equation}
The prescribed conditions are $\boldsymbol{U}=(1,0.4,0.2)$, $\boldsymbol{g}=(0,-0.3,0.1)$, $\boldsymbol{u}_d(0)=(0.2,-0.1,0.05)$, and $\boldsymbol{x}_d(0)=\boldsymbol{0}$. These conditions produce a time-dependent droplet velocity while retaining an analytical solution for the trajectory. Temporal convergence is evaluated using the Euclidean error in the droplet position at the final time:
\begin{equation}
    e_x =
    \left\|
    \boldsymbol{x}_{\mathrm{num}}(t_f)
    -
    \boldsymbol{x}_{\mathrm{exact}}(t_f)
    \right\|
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:app_lagrangian_temporal_order} shows the decrease in the final-position error with time-step refinement. The error follows the second-order reference slope, confirming second-order temporal convergence of the Lagrangian trajectory integration for this verification problem.

\section{Level-Set Advection}
\label{app:levelset_verification}

The spatial and temporal accuracy of the level-set transport solver is assessed using the uniform translation of a circular level-set field over the unit-square domain $\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]$. The field has a radius $R=0.2$ and is initially centered at $(0.35,0.45)$. A constant velocity $\boldsymbol{V}=(0.2,0.1,0)$ is prescribed from $t=0$ to $t=0.5$, resulting in a displacement of $(0.1,0.05)$ and a final center position of $(0.45,0.50)$. Since the field undergoes pure translation without deformation, the analytical solution at the final time is obtained directly by translating the initial field.

\subsection{Spatial Convergence}

Spatial convergence is evaluated using four successively refined meshes with characteristic spacings $h=\Delta x=\Delta y=0.03125$, $0.015625$, $0.0078125$, and $0.00390625$, corresponding to $N=32$, $64$, $128$, and $256$ divisions along each coordinate direction. The time step is reduced consistently with mesh refinement such that the temporal error remains negligible relative to the spatial discretization error. Figure~\ref{fig:app_ls_verification}(a) shows the decrease in the $L_2$ error with mesh refinement. The observed convergence approaches second order, consistent with the second-order upwind scheme used by the level-set solver.

\subsection{Temporal Convergence}

Temporal convergence is evaluated at a fixed spatial resolution of $N=256$. Starting from $\Delta t=0.03125$, the time step is successively halved, corresponding to $16$, $32$, $64$, $128$, $256$, and $512$ time steps over the prescribed final time. The spatial discretization is kept fixed throughout the refinement study. The second-order Rosenbrock--Wanner scheme is used to advance the level-set advection equation. To isolate the temporal discretization error from the fixed spatial error, the numerical solutions are compared with a reference solution computed on the same spatial mesh using $2048$ time steps. Figure~\ref{fig:app_ls_verification}(b) shows the resulting $L_2$ error with time-step refinement. The measured convergence remains second order, consistent with the formal temporal accuracy of the two stage Rosenbrock--Wanner scheme.

\section{Eulerian Immersed-Boundary Droplet Formulation}
\label{app:eulerian_ibm_convergence}

The spatial convergence of the Eulerian immersed-boundary droplet formulation is assessed using the analytical cylinder impingement problem introduced in Section~\ref{ibm:sec:cylinder}. The Cartesian background mesh is successively refined from $h=0.04$ to $h=0.0025$, where $h=\Delta x$ denotes the uniform Cartesian cell size, while all other numerical parameters are maintained constant. The convergence is evaluated using the error in the integrated collection efficiency $\beta_{\mathrm{tot}}$ relative to the analytical solution.

Figure~\ref{fig:app_eulerian_ibm_convergence} shows the decrease in the integrated collection-efficiency error with mesh refinement. The observed convergence is approximately second order. A similar behavior was reported by Lavoie et al.~\cite{lavoieDroplets} for immersed-boundary Eulerian droplet calculations. Although volume penalization generally limits the spatial accuracy near the immersed boundary, the penalization of the droplet equations is applied within the shadow region, where the collection efficiency is zero, rather than within the impingement region where $\beta$ is evaluated. Consequently, the penalization does not necessarily limit the convergence rate of the predicted collection efficiency.
